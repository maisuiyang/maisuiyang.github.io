Product Overview

PalUp is a mobile app concept that helps people find company for meals through shared dining events, participant profiles, in-app chat, and filters for cuisine and vibe.

The idea came from a simple insight: choosing where to eat is usually easier than choosing who to go with. People care about social fit, comfort, and knowing what they are signing up for, not just food preferences.

For this project, we scoped the prototype to Seattle and focused on helping users discover dining events, understand who is attending, join a group meal, and coordinate plans through chat.

The problem

Problem

Finding dining companions is harder than it should be, so many people still end up eating alone even when they would be open to sharing a meal.

Who feels it most

People who are new to a city or traveling solo, especially when their local social networks are still forming.

Research question

What barriers prevent people from finding others to eat with, and what would make dining with strangers feel comfortable? Could shared meals help people build social connections over time?

Research

Methods
We started with a screening survey, then conducted six semi-structured interviews about dining alone, dining with others, and what would make shared dining feel comfortable.
Findings
Participants often brought up trust with strangers, scheduling, and whether conversation would feel natural. Some also said they eat alone for practical reasons, not necessarily because they feel lonely.
Takeaway
Most participants were open to dining with others, but the main barriers were coordination and uncertainty about who they would meet. Smaller groups, clearer event details, and more transparent participant information made people more willing to join.

Testing

Outcome

The final outcome is an interactive Figma prototype for PalUp, a dining companion app that helps people discover dining events in Seattle, see who is attending, join group meals, and coordinate logistics through chat.

The prototype also includes profile and account flows that support trust and personalization, such as profile previews, clearer confirmation feedback, settings management, and Help & Support access.

Challenges & Learnings

Challenges

  • Onboarding balance: Collecting enough profile information for matching and trust while keeping setup quick.
  • Trust before commitment: Helping users understand who they might meet before joining an event.
  • Information clarity: Presenting event, chat, and profile details without overwhelming the screen.
  • Cross-feature consistency: Making event, chat, and profile flows feel like one connected product.
  • Iteration under constraints: Prioritizing fixes that improved task completion, feedback, and next-step clarity within limited testing cycles.

Learnings

This project showed me that UX is more than visual polish. It requires understanding users, testing assumptions, and improving decisions with evidence from heuristic evaluation and usability testing.

As a CS student, I learned that a feature can work on paper but still fail users if the flow is confusing. Usability knowledge helps me collaborate better with design and product teams and catch interaction issues earlier.
